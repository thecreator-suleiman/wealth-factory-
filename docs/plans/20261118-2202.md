# Free business plan: Michael Dell's $1,000 computer business

### The idea in one line
Buy standard computer parts, assemble them to order, and sell reliable PCs locally with a personal warranty.

### Who pays and why
* **Who pays:** Students, gamers, remote workers, and small business owners.
* **Why:** Brand-name computers are expensive and often overpriced. Customers pay you because they get a custom-built, high-performing PC tailored to their exact needs for less money, plus friendly local support if something breaks.

### Start-up cost (low/realistic, in USD)
* **Toolkit (screwdrivers, anti-static wrist strap, zip ties):** $30
* **Test monitor, keyboard, and mouse (used):** $40
* **First inventory (parts for one entry-level PC):** $450
* **Marketing (flyers, mobile data):** $30
* **Total Start-up Cost:** **$550** *(You can start even lower if your first customer pays a 50% deposit upfront).*

### Step-by-step: first 30 days
* **Days 1–5:** Research popular budget PC builds online. Find trusted local or international online parts suppliers.
* **Days 6–10:** Create simple social media pages (WhatsApp Business, Instagram, Facebook). Post about custom builds coming soon.
* **Days 11–20:** Order parts for your very first demo machine. Assemble it slowly and carefully while recording videos for social media. Install a free operating system.
* **Days 21–30:** Show the working PC to friends, family, and local community boards. Secure your first paying client.

### How to get the first 10 customers
* **Leverage WhatsApp:** Status updates are free marketing. Post photos of parts arriving and machines lighting up.
* **Visit local campuses:** Drop off simple flyers or business cards on notice boards at universities and high schools.
* **Offer referral bonuses:** Tell your first customer you will give them $10 cash for every friend they send your way who buys a PC.
* **Target small offices:** Accountants, graphic designers, and internet cafes often need reliable desktop upgrades.

### Simple pricing example
* **Cost of parts:** $500
* **Your time and labor markup (20%):** $100
* **Final Selling Price:** $600
* **Your Profit:** $100 per computer.

### Risks and how to avoid them
* **Risk:** A part arrives broken or fails during assembly.
  * *Fix:* Only buy parts that come with a manufacturer warranty and keep your anti-static workspace safe.
* **Risk:** Customers demand refunds if they change their minds.
  * *Fix:* Use a simple written sales agreement stating parts are custom-ordered and non-refundable, but include a 30-day hardware repair warranty to build trust.
* **Risk:** Cash flow gets stuck in unsold inventory.
  * *Fix:* Never build spec computers with your own money until you have a confirmed order and at least a 50% deposit from the buyer.

### Free tools to use
* **PCPartPicker.com:** To check if your chosen computer parts are compatible before buying.
* **YouTube:** The ultimate free university for learning how to build PCs step-by-step.
* **Canva:** To design simple, professional price lists and flyers for your phone.
* **WhatsApp Business:** To chat with clients, send quotes, and manage orders.

Not financial advice - do your own research.
