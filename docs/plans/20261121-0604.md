# Free guide: Wise vs traditional banks remittance fees

## What it is

Sending money across borders is usually expensive. Traditional banks charge high hidden fees and use poor exchange rates. Wise is a digital money transfer tool designed to fix this. It lets you send and receive money internationally using the real mid-market exchange rate—the same rate you see on Google. 

## Is it really free (and the catch)

Wise is free to sign up for and hold money in. However, sending money is **not** 100% free. 

The catch is that Wise charges a small, transparent fee for every transfer. This fee depends on the currencies and payment methods involved. Despite this fee, Wise is almost always much cheaper than traditional banks because banks hide their fees inside terrible exchange rates. 

## How to sign up

1. Go to the official website: [wise.com](https://wise.com)
2. Click "Register" or "Sign up" using your email, Google, or Apple account.
3. Choose a "Personal" or "Business" account.
4. Enter your country of residence and verify your phone number.
5. Upload a government-issued ID (passport or national ID) to verify your identity. 

## 5 steps to get value in your first week

1. **Compare a transfer:** Enter an amount you want to send on Wise and check the total cost compared to your local bank. 
2. **Add a recipient:** Save the bank details of the person or business you want to pay.
3. **Fund a small test transfer:** Send a small amount first to see how fast and smooth the process is.
4. **Order a Wise debit card:** If available in your country, get the physical card to spend money globally with low fees.
5. **Set up rate alerts:** Turn on notifications to see when your currency exchange rate improves.

## Tips

* **Always check the mid-market rate:** Before sending money anywhere else, check Google for the real exchange rate. 
* **Pay via bank transfer:** Funding your Wise transfer with a debit card is fast, but bank transfers usually have lower fees.
* **Avoid double conversion:** Try to send money in the local currency of the receiving country to avoid extra conversion fees.
* **Check availability:** Wise features vary by country, especially across Africa. Check if your specific country supports both sending and receiving.

## Free alternatives

* **Revolut:** Great for multi-currency holding and spending, though availability in Africa is currently limited.
* **Sendwave:** Popular for fast, low-cost remittances to certain African countries.
* **Remitly:** Often offers great promotional exchange rates for your very first transfer.
* **Cryptocurrency stablecoins (USDT/USDC):** Can be very cheap for global transfers, but require technical setup and local cash-out options.

Not financial advice - do your own research.
